A tiny autoencoder

A tiny autoencoder is built and trained on a laptop on self-generated pictures of one square or disc, and its loss sits on a blank-page plateau for four epochs before dropping. A linear probe reads the shape's position from the eight-number code with R squared near 0.95, almost as well as from raw pixels. A two-dimensional map of the code keeps position but loses size.

Contrastive learning, then collapse on purpose

A miniature SimCLR is built from two augmented views per picture, an encoder and the InfoNCE loss. With negatives, the embeddings use nearly all eight directions and a probe reads the shape almost perfectly; without negatives, they collapse to one grey point with a perfect loss. A variance term restores each bead's spread, but only along about three directions.

A mini-JEPA

A mini-JEPA hides blocks of a patch grid and predicts their embeddings with a predictor and a slow twin. Adding an action and cross-entropy-method planning brings the shape about 1.4 pixels from the goal, against 3.9 for doing nothing. The probe finds position neither in the averaged embeddings nor, beyond a random twin, in the full map.

A JEPA needs something to hide, so the first cell cuts every picture into patches. Four by four pixels each, which makes a grid of eight by eight: sixty-four patches. Think of the picture as a map, cut into sixty-four little squares. Every patch will get its own necklace of eight beads.

Each one covers between fifteen and twenty percent of the grid, in a random rectangle. Then it picks one large context block, covering between eighty-five and a hundred percent, and removes the targets from it. On the map, the targets are four torn-out corners, and the context is what remains. In the example on the screen, thirty-three patches are hidden, and thirty-one are seen. The encoder is a very small transformer, from episode three point five. Each patch becomes a vector, a position is added so that it knows which square it is, and two layers of attention mix the patches. A last layer writes one necklace of eight beads for each patch. It has seventy-two thousand, seven hundred seventy-six parameters. It receives only the patches it is given, so the context encoder sees the context, and nothing else.

The predictor is the lantern, PREDI. It is a small transformer too. It receives the necklaces of the visible patches, and, for every hidden patch, an empty token that carries only a position. Then the slow twin. It starts as an exact copy of the encoder, and no gradient ever reaches it. After every step, it moves a little toward the encoder: that is the exponential moving average. The momentum starts at zero point nine nine six, and rises to one over the training, which is the schedule of the I-JEPA paper. At one, the twin stops moving. The twin looks at the whole picture, and its necklaces on the hidden patches are the targets.

Now the loss, inside the training loop. The twin writes the targets. The context encoder reads the visible patches, the predictor guesses the hidden ones, and the loss is the mean squared difference between guess and target.
